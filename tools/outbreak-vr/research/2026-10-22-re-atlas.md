# Resident Evil Outbreak File #1: an RE atlas for one-to-one VR (2026-10-22)

Alex, 2026-10-21: "I want this to really feel like a one-to-one experience... Resident Evil Outbreak VR, not something
thrown over the top." This note does three things:
1. It audits every VR feature in the build: the game's own, or ours on top.
2. It maps the game systems we have not touched yet, as far as this pass got.
3. It ranks the eight reverse-engineering conversions that would move the build the most.

How this was made: read-only and static. It uses the J's Bar RAM dump (`ui/ram_now.bin`) and the savestates already
extracted to the session scratchpad (`truevr/f1_0N`), never committed. No emulator, no builds and no game writes. It
builds on every note in this folder (`2026-10-21-true-stereo.md` is on branch `truestereo`, `-vr-presence.md` on
`vrpresence`, `-hand-pickup.md` on `handpickup`, `-grab-first-person.md` on `grabdoor`) and on the 2026-10-15 review.

Labels:
- **verified (code)**: read in the disassembly;
- **verified (memory)**: read in a dump;
- **assumed**: reasoned, not checked;
- **open**: not mapped yet.

**Coverage, stated plainly:** five parallel static surveys (sound, animation, HUD and event scripts, AI senses,
collision) were still running when this note had to be committed. Their scratch output is in
`scratchpad/reatlas/<area>/`, but their findings are **not** in this version. §2 holds what this pass verified itself,
plus the known addresses and the exact next steps for each system. A follow-up should fold the surveys in.

## 1. The "on top" audit

**Labels used in this section:**
- **Game:** the game's own code or assets do it (we may patch a word or drive an input).
- **Hybrid:** the game's code decides the outcome, but our approximation feeds it.
- **On top:** our own drawing, input mapping or estimate.

**Headline:** the table has 33 rows: 7 Game, 8 Hybrid (the game decides and we feed it) and 18 on top. Of the 18,
8 belong on top: turning and comfort, reprojection, FOV, the menu room hold, gamma, the debug box, body follow and
walk-and-shoot, which is deliberate. The other 10 are what reads as "thrown over the top":
1. the mono picture on a screen;
2. the CC0 hands and the impostor weapons, drawn by us even though the meshes are the game's;
3. the laser, the muzzle flash and the recoil;
4. the haptic guesses;
5. the wrist card;
6. the toasts and the pickup cue;
7. the zone markers;
8. the presence shell.

The constant eye height hides inside the head-camera hybrid. Every one of these now has a game-side replacement named,
mapped (the shot, the head bone, the hand bone, the near cull, the prompt) or with its next step written down (sound,
the HUD text pass, scripts, collision).

## 2. Untouched game systems: what is mapped

### New in this pass (verified)

**The shot, end to end** (`FUN_0062CF10(record s2, item index s1)`, verified (code)):

| Address | What |
|---|---|
| `0x62CF54`-`0x62CF78` | Returns unless the weapon type (`+0xCA8`) entry in `0x6E5940` has flag `0x10` (a gun). |
| `0x62CF98` `lw s0,0(v1)` | `s0` = the item record's weapon object (`0x3967EC + 60*item`, i.e. item `+0x1C`): the gun model in the hand. **The hook point.** |
| `0x62CFA4` | `0x634260(rec, 5)` (assumed: the shot's motion or state). |
| `0x62CFD0`-`0x62D004` | Muzzle = type entry `+0x34` (at `0x6E5940+0x38`) times the object's matrix `+0x1C0`, plus its position `+0x200`. |
| `0x62D008`-`0x62D044` | Direction = identity turned by gp constants -π/2 (`0x239E94`) and +π/2 (`0x239CDC`) (`0x18FB70`, `0x18FAD0`), times the object's matrix: the model's barrel becomes the attack shape's +Z. |
| `0x62D064` | Stance 5 (grabbed) takes a separate branch (assumed: a shot while grabbed). |
| `0x62D140`-`0x62D1C8` | The origin moves forward along the shot by a per-character, per-class f32 (`0x6E6D30 + class*32 + char*4`). It is a fixed offset, not random spread. |
| `0x62D1CC` | `FUN_005521B0(rec, matrix, origin, item)` spawns the attack object (type 1, 3 frames, the 10 m capsule ray of `2026-10-14-melee.md`). |
| `0x62D1D8` | `0x1B7310(rec, 0)`: for a human it calls `0x1B7A70(slot+8, kind, ?, rec+0x38, 1, 0, 0)` at `0x1B73C4`. Assumed: the gunshot sound effect, **positioned at the character's feet position**. |
| (the gun-flag branch) | Assumed effects: `0x6801A0(rec, obj+0x200)`, `0x5AC8D0(rec, matrix, origin, class)` (likely the muzzle flash), `0x55E400(rec, obj+0x1C0, type-1)` (likely the shell), `0x682090(rec, 0)`. The last one marks the item record (`+0x09`, `+0x28`) and reads per-character tables. |

### (a) Sound: open

**Known:**
- We have no audio code at all (`pcsx2/VR` has none). Everything heard is the game's own SPU2 mix in stereo.
- The one positional call found is the shot's `0x1B7A70(channel = slot+8, id, ?, emitter = record+0x38, ...)`. It
  takes a pointer to an emitter position, so the volume and pan are almost certainly computed on the EE against a
  listener. **assumed**
- The listener is the open question:
  - If it is the camera eye `0x306338` or view `0x3062E0`: since first person writes the camera from the head, sound is
    already head-relative in yaw (30 fps, about 100 ms late).
  - If it is the character's heading `+0x92`: it is body-relative.
- The unidentified `0x5400A0(2, 40, 60)` after the door shot is the other lead.

**Next:**
1. Disassemble `0x1B7A70` and the table it fills.
2. Find the per-frame pass that reads those emitter pointers and calls sqrt, atan or sin-cos, and see what it subtracts
   (the eye or `+0x38`).
3. Find the SIF RPC that carries the result to the IOP; the module names are in `iopMemory.bin`.

**One-to-one options:**
- (1) Patch the listener read to the VR eye. It is one word if it reads the camera, which first person already writes,
  so it may already be right.
- (2) For true HRTF, the host reads the emitter table each vsync and spatialises per voice. That needs a per-voice
  split of the SPU2 mix, which PCSX2 does not offer today, so it is weeks.
- (1) first.

**Co-op:** local only. Every console mixes its own sound.

### (b) Animation: partly known

**Known:**
- Channel 0 at record `+0x1E4`: f32 frame `+0x00`, end `+0x04`, motion id `+0x0E`. Motions run at 60 frames a second.
  **verified**
- Motion event scripts: `FUN_00543410` -> `FUN_005654D0`, events through `FUN_00565C80`. **verified**
- Bone structs: `*(rec+0x34)+0x820+4*bone`, 0x190 bytes. `+0x00` world, `+0x40` local, `+0x80` inverse bind, `+0xC8`
  parent. World = local x parent world, concatenated later in the frame. **verified**
- The game's own per-bone local rotation: `FUN_0055C450` -> `FUN_00340210`/`FUN_001903E0`, run from the zombie update
  (`0x6644D0`). It lands before the concatenation. **verified**
- Skin matrices are built in the draw by `FUN_001922B0`. **verified**
- The held object's matrix follows the hand bone every frame, even with its draw skipped
  (`2026-10-14-weapon-models-in-hand.md`). **verified**

**For `vrbody`:** it rewrites world matrices just before the draw (`0x345314`). By then the weapon object, the melee
attack object (`FUN_00552CF0` copies the weapon object in the update) and the hurt spheres have already been computed
from the animation's pose. So the drawn arm would hold the VR pose while the game's weapon and its hits stay at the
canned pose. **assumed from that order**

**The one-to-one hook** is the local matrices (`+0x40`) of the arm and head bones, written after the animation sample
and before the hierarchy concatenation, in the update. That is where `FUN_0055C450` already writes. Then:
- the world matrices are right;
- the weapon object is in the VR hand, and the game draws it with its own lights;
- melee and shots need no caves A, B or the new shot cave.

**Open:**
- the sampler and blend function, and the clip format;
- the concatenation call and its place in the character loop `FUN_00638850`;
- whether the game has an aim or look layer (the torso pitch from `+0xBC8`) to drive instead of raw IK.

**Co-op:** partners receive action bytes, position, heading, aim and HP (type 1 and 2 messages, `FUN_0064C620`), never
bones. A VR pose is local, and partners see it only through `VrPresence`'s channel.
